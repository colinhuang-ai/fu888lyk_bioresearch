## Abstract

While third‐generation EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) improve outcomes in EGFR‐mutant NSCLC, optimal selection between these agents remains uncertain. This real‐world study compares the efficacy and safety of aumolertinib with those of osimertinib in the first‐line treatment setting. We retrospectively analyzed 109 EGFR‐mutant (exon 19del/L858R) NSCLC patients treated with first‐line aumolertinib (*n* = 53) or osimertinib (*n* = 56) across four tertiary hospitals (2016–2023). The primary end points included progression‐free survival (PFS) and safety; secondary end points encompassed overall survival (OS), objective response rate (ORR), and predictive biomarker identification through subgroup analyses. Aumolertinib demonstrated superior median PFS compared to osimertinib (34.4 vs. 26.9 months; HR 0.52, 95% CI 0.29–0.92, *p* = 0.031), with comparable OS (HR 0.76, 95% CI 0.27–2.15, *p* = 0.60). No significant differences emerged in primary lesion ORR (33.96%, 18/53 vs. 42.86%, 24/56) or adverse event (AE) rates (37.7%, 20/53 vs. 39.3%, 22/56). Subgroup analyses confirmed enhanced PFS benefit with aumolertinib in exon 19del‐mutant (HR 0.43, 95% CI 0.21–0.87, *p* = 0.02) and co‐mutation‐negative (HR 0.53, 95% CI 0.25–1.14, *p* = 0.08) populations. Subgroup analysis identified age < 65 years (HR 0.46, 95% CI 0.22–0.99, *p* = 0.046), brain metastases (HR 0.24, 95% CI 0.09–0.66, *p* = 0.006), and exon 19del status (HR 0.43, 95% CI 0.2–0.89, *p* = 0.023) as independent PFS predictors. Regarding safety, aumolertinib showed a lower overall incidence of liver dysfunction compared to osimertinib (9.43%, 5/53 vs. 14.29%, 8/56), with Grade ≥ 3 events being rare (1.89%, 1/53). Given that liver function is a critical determinant of prognosis, the manageable hepatic safety profile of aumolertinib supports its long‐term clinical utility. Our study demonstrated a clinically meaningful PFS advantage of aumolertinib over osimertinib in treatment‐naïve EGFR‐mutant NSCLC, particularly for exon 19del‐driven and co‐mutation‐negative disease, while maintaining equivalent safety. These findings provide valuable real‐world evidence supporting the use of aumolertinib as a first‐line therapy, contributing to the optimization of treatment selection and AE management. While these subgroup‐specific results offer hypothesis‐generating insights into potential biomarkers for therapeutic individualization, these preliminary findings require further validation in larger, prospective cohorts.

## Introduction

Non‐small cell lung adenocarcinoma, the most common histological subtype of lung cancer, represents 40%–50% of non‐small cell lung carcinoma (NSCLC) cases [[1]], with a significant global burden reflected in 2 million new diagnoses worldwide in 2022, including 730,000 cases and 610,000 deaths in China (nearly one‐third of the global total) [[2]]. Primary risk factors encompass tobacco smoking (linked to 85%–90% of lung cancers), air pollution, and occupational carcinogens (e.g., asbestos, arsenic); however, compared to squamous cell carcinoma, lung adenocarcinoma shows a weaker direct association with smoking and predominates in women and never‐smokers [[3]]. Stage‐specific therapeutic approaches include surgical resection for early‐stage disease (achieving a 60%–70% five‐year survival rate), neoadjuvant PD‐1 inhibitor‐based chemoimmunotherapy for locally advanced cases (yielding ~60% major pathological response), and EGFR‐targeted therapy (e.g., osimertinib for exon 19del/L858R mutations) in advanced stages, which provides a median progression‐free survival (PFS) of 26.9 months [[4]]. Upon resistance development, T790M mutation testing guides subsequent therapy adjustments, while anti‐angiogenic agents like bevacizumab or conventional chemoradiation remain options for driver‐negative or extensively metastatic tumors, underscoring the importance of precision medicine in optimizing outcomes [[5]].

EGFR‐TKIs (epidermal growth factor receptor tyrosine kinase inhibitors) have evolved through three generations, substantially enhancing clinical outcomes for NSCLC patients. First‐generation drugs like gefitinib and erlotinib target EGFR‐sensitive mutations (19del/L858R), achieving a median PFS of 9–13 months; however, approximately 50%–60% of patients develop resistance due to T790M mutations [[6]]. Second‐generation agents such as afatinib and dacomitinib irreversibly inhibit EGFR and show efficacy against rare mutations like G719X, though their clinical application is constrained by higher toxicity profiles, including severe diarrhea and rash [[7]]. The third‐generation inhibitor osimertinib overcomes T790M‐mediated resistance, extends median PFS to 18.9 months in first‐line therapy, and demonstrates marked efficacy against brain metastases while maintaining superior safety, with grade ≥ 3 adverse events (AE) occurring in less than 10% of patients [[8]].

Aumolertinib (Ameile), a domestically developed third‐generation epidermal growth factor receptor tyrosine kinase inhibitor (EGFR‐TKI) in China, delivers targeted therapeutic effects against advanced NSCLC through selective inhibition of EGFR‐sensitive mutations (exon 19 deletion/L858R) and T790M resistance mutations [[9]]. Structural optimization via cyclopropyl substituent incorporation enhances its blood–brain barrier (BBB) permeability, with clinical studies demonstrating a tumor control rate of 61% in cerebral metastasis patients—closely aligning with osimertinib's reported 66% control rate [[10]]. The multicenter randomized Phase III AENEAS trial demonstrated that aumolertinib, as a first‐line treatment for Chinese NSCLC patients, achieved a median PFS of 19.3 months (HR 0.319; *p* < 0.0001) and a CNS median PFS of 29.0 months (HR 0.319; *p* < 0.0001) [[11]]. All efficacy data were derived exclusively from the Chinese population, ensuring relevance to the genetic characteristics of Asian populations. Safety evaluations revealed grade ≥ 3 treatment‐related AEs occurred in merely 7% of treated patients, with a 40% reduction in diarrhea incidence compared to osimertinib and absence of interstitial lung disease (ILD) cases, underscoring its improved tolerability profile relative to earlier EGFR‐TKI generations. The agent's mechanism of action combines potent inhibition of T790M‐mediated resistance mutations with minimized wild‐type EGFR binding, thereby attenuating off‐target toxicities such as cutaneous reactions. Despite concurrent approval of aumolertinib and osimertinib as first‐line NSCLC therapies, a paucity of head‐to‐head comparative studies persists regarding their efficacy and safety differentials. This retrospective cohort analysis systematically evaluates comparative clinical outcomes and safety parameters between these agents, aiming to establish an evidence hierarchy to inform optimized therapeutic decision‐making in EGFR mutation‐positive NSCLC management. Although the landmark FLAURA and AENEAS trials established Osimertinib and Aumolertinib as first‐line standards of care, their stringent inclusion criteria may not fully reflect the complexity of real‐world clinical profiles, particularly regarding heterogeneous co‐mutation statuses. Furthermore, while third‐generation TKIs are recommended by guidelines, the current therapeutic paradigm remains somewhat generalized. In the era of precision oncology, there is an urgent need to refine treatment strategies beyond a ‘one‐size‐fits‐all’ approach by considering granular factors such as exon 19del versus L858R mutations, co‐mutation presence, and specific metastatic sites like the brain or liver.

Currently, direct head‐to‐head evidence comparing these two agents in real‐world settings is scarce. Therefore, this study aims to bridge this clinical evidence gap by comparing the real‐world PFS of Aumolertinib and Osimertinib, with a focused exploration of their differential performance in exon 19del and co‐mutation‐negative subgroups, thereby providing a more scientific basis for individualized treatment optimization.

## Materials and Methods

### Patients

This multicenter retrospective observational study analyzed treatment‐naïve patients with histologically confirmed non‐squamous NSCLC who received first‐line therapy with osimertinib (80 mg/day) or aumolertinib (110 mg/day) at four tertiary hospitals in Lanzhou between January 2016 and March 2023, with inclusion criteria requiring: (1) age > 18 years; (2) pathologically verified non‐squamous NSCLC diagnosis; (3) documented TNM staging; (4) EGFR‐sensitive mutations confirmed by molecular testing; (5) completion of ≥ 1 treatment cycle; (6) ≥ 1 measurable lesion per RECIST v1.1 criteria; (7) complete clinical records, while excluding patients receiving second‐line or subsequent targeted therapy, those with nonlocally advanced disease, and cases with EGFR mutation subtypes other than exon 19 deletion or L858R. This selection bias control aimed to minimize selection bias and reduce clinical heterogeneity from the source. To ensure data homogeneity across participating centers, all sites followed standardized diagnostic, therapeutic, and follow‐up protocols. Clinical data were rigorously screened for completeness of follow‐up records to minimize inter‐center variability.

### Intervention

All enrolled patients received first‐line treatment with osimertinib or aumolertinib, with initial dosages standardized at 80 mg/day for osimertinib and 110 mg/day for aumolertinib. Therapeutic strategy adjustments were mandated upon detection of disease progression (RECIST v1.1) or emergence of grade ≥ 3 AEs (CTCAE v5.0).

### Study End points

The primary end points of this study were PFS and safety profile, with secondary end points comprising OS and objective response rate (ORR). These were the prespecified objectives of the study protocol. In contrast, all subgroup analyses and prognostic evaluations were conducted as exploratory analyses to generate hypotheses regarding therapeutic optimization. PFS was defined as the duration from treatment initiation to either disease progression (per RECIST 1.1), loss to follow‐up, or study cutoff. OS was calculated from the first dose administration until death from any cause, loss to follow‐up, or final data collection. ORR was determined as the proportion of complete responses (CR) and partial responses (PR) among evaluable patients relative to the total assessable cohort. Tumor response to aumolertinib or osimertinib was systematically assessed according to the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors version 1.1 (RECIST 1.1). AEs were graded using the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for AEs version 4.03 (CTCAE v4.03).

### Statistical Analysis

Categorical data were presented as counts with corresponding percentages, while continuous measures were characterized by mean ± standard deviation (SD) for parametric distributions or median with interquartile range (IQR) for nonparametric datasets. Intergroup comparisons of continuous variables utilized Student's *t*‐test (two‐group comparisons) or one‐way analysis of variance (ANOVA) for multi‐group analyses. Comparative evaluation of categorical variables employed *χ* <sup>2</sup> tests, with Fisher's exact test implementation when expected frequencies fell below 5. Survival trajectories for PFS and OS were graphically represented through Kaplan–Meier estimators, with between‐arm divergence significance determined via log‐rank testing. Cox proportional hazards regression modeling was implemented to quantify covariate effects on survival end points, expressed through hazard ratio (HR) with 95% confidence intervals. All analyses were conducted using the R statistical computing environment (v4.3.1; R Core Team 2023), with two‐tailed *p* values < 0.05 establishing statistical significance throughout the study's analytical paradigm.

### Efficacy End points

The median follow‐up was 22.87 months (Q1–Q3, 19.63–29.87) for the Aumolertinib group and 23.80 months (95% CI 17.19–33.26) for the Osimertinib group, with PFS serving as the primary endpoint. Patients treated with Aumolertinib exhibited a significantly prolonged median PFS of 34.4 months (95% CI 30.9–not reached [NR]) versus 26.9 months (95% CI 22.2–40.0) in the Osimertinib group, corresponding to a 48% reduction in progression risk (HR 0.52, 95% CI 0.29–0.92, *p* = 0.031) (Figure 1B). This advantage was further substantiated by higher 1‐year (94%, 48/53 vs. 91%, 48/56), 2‐year (76%, 22/53 vs. 54%, 21/56) and 3‐year (48%, 4/53 vs. 36%, 8/56) PFS rates in the Aumolertinib group (Table S1).

In patients with EGFR exon 19 deletions, aumolertinib demonstrated a statistically significant improvement in median PFS compared to osimertinib (34.4 months, 95% CI 30.6–NA vs. 23.7 months, 95% CI 19.9–NA, HR 0.43, 95% CI 0.21–0.87, *p* = 0.020; Figure 2A). Conversely, among EGFR L858R‐mutated patients, mPFS was NR for aumolertinib versus 31.2 months (95% CI 23.0–NA) for osimertinib, with no significant difference observed (HR 0.76, 95% CI 0.26–2.23, *p* = 0.62; Figure 2B). In co‐mutated subgroups, the mPFS was 30.6 months (95% CI 16.0–NA) for aumolertinib and 23.7 months (95% CI 17.6–NA) for osimertinib, though this difference did not reach statistical significance (HR 0.68, 95% CI 0.25–1.84, *p* = 0.48; Figure 2C). Notably, non‐co‐mutated patients treated with aumolertinib exhibited a numerically prolonged mPFS (34.4 months [95% CI 30.9–NA] vs. 27.6 months [95% CI 23.0–NA]; HR 0.53, 95% CI 0.25–1.14, *p* = 0.08), suggesting a trend toward clinical benefit (Figure 2D).

With osimertinib as the reference treatment, univariate Cox regression analysis demonstrated statistically significant PFS improvement in the overall population (HR 0.51, 95% CI 0.27–0.95, *p* = 0.034) [[2,5]]. Stratified analyses revealed differential efficacy patterns: patients aged < 65 years showed marked benefit (HR 0.46, 95% CI 0.22–0.99, *p* = 0.046), while those ≥ 65 years exhibited non‐significant trends (HR 0.63, 95% CI 0.21–0.91, *p* = 0.413). Notably, the brain metastasis subgroup achieved a 76% risk reduction (HR 0.24, 95% CI 0.09–0.66, *p* = 0.006), contrasting with liver metastasis cases where statistical significance was not attained (HR 0.39, 95% CI 0.06–2.47, *p* = 0.317). Molecular stratification identified robust efficacy in exon 19 deletion (19del) mutation carriers (HR 0.43, 95% CI 0.2–0.89, *p* = 0.023), whereas L858R mutation subgroups showed neutral response (HR 0.74, 95% CI 0.23–2.4, *p* = 0.621). Numerical PFS advantages were observed in patients without liver metastasis (HR 0.53,95% CI 0.27–1.04, *p* = 0.066) and the co‐mutation‐negative population (HR 0.51,95% CI 0.24–1.11, *p* = 0.089). Although these findings did not reach formal statistical significance, they provide hypothesis‐generating insights into potential therapeutic optimization that requires further validation in larger cohorts. These results establish preferential efficacy in younger patients, those with brain metastases, and 19del mutation carriers, while underscoring the imperative for powered confirmatory studies in older individuals, L858R mutation subgroups, and liver metastasis cohorts. The secondary end points comprised OS and ORR. Median OS was NR with aumolertinib versus 53.8 months (95% CI 38.7–NA) for osimertinib, showing no significant between‐group difference (HR 0.76, 95% CI 0.27–2.15, *p* = 0.60, data not shown). ### AEs

Comparative safety analysis between aumolertinib (*N* = 53) and osimertinib (*N* = 56) revealed no statistically significant disparities in AE profiles. Overall, any‐grade AEs were reported in 37.74% (20/53) and 39.29% (22/56) of patients, while grade ≥ 3 events occurred at nearly identical rates (3.77%, 2/53 vs. 3.57%, 2/56). The predominant AEs included hepatobiliary dysfunction (9.43%, 5/53 vs. 14.29%, 8/53), creatine kinase elevation (7.55%, 4/53 vs. 1.79%, 1/56), and dermatologic reactions (3.77%, 2/53 vs. 3.57%, 2/56), with comparable severity distributions between the two groups. Notably, grade ≥ 3 toxicities were infrequent and limited to hepatic impairment (1.89%, 1/53) and rash (1.89%, 1/53) in the aumolertinib group, versus hematologic suppression (1.79%, 1/56) and acral erythema (1.79%, 1/56) with osimertinib, all demonstrating nonsignificant intergroup variance (*p* > 0.05) (Table 2).

### Prognosis and Post‐Resistance

The comparative analysis of EGFR‐mutated NSCLC patients revealed significantly reduced disease progression with aumolertinib versus osimertinib (progression rates: 22.64% [12/53] vs. 55.36% [31/56]; *p* < 0.001), corresponding to progression‐free rates of 77.36% (41/53) and 44.64% (25/56), respectively. Among progressed cases, progression patterns showed no intergroup disparity (*p* = 0.849): intracranial progression occurred in 8.33% (1/12) of aumolertinib‐treated patients compared to 12.90% (4/31) with osimertinib, while extracranial progression predominated in both cohorts (75.00% [9/12] vs. 77.42% [24/31]). Concurrent intracranial/extracranial progression was observed in 16.67% (2/12) versus 9.68% (3/31) of cases. These findings underscore aumolertinib's superior progression control despite comparable anatomical resistance patterns between the two agents (Figure 4A). Quantitative survival analysis demonstrated differential progression between treatment groups, with Aumolertinib‐treated patients exhibiting isolated intracranial progression (8.33%, 1/12) sustained through 30‐month PFS prior to mortality at 55‐month OS, contrasting with osimertinib‐treated counterparts showing higher neuroaxis failure rates (12.9%, 4/31) and variable therapeutic durability (PFS 5–35 months) culminating in one 35‐month OS mortality event (Figure 4C). Extracranial progression dynamics revealed pharmacologically distinct patterns: 75% (9/12) of Aumolertinib patients developed visceral metastases following LOT1 therapy (PFS 10–35 months) with singlemortality at 11‐month OS, while osimertinib‐treated cohorts demonstrated broader resistance mechanisms (77.42%, 24/31) with extended PFS windows (5–50 months) and temporally clustered mortality events spanning 13–40 months OS (Figure 4B). Notably, bifocal progression analyses uncovered therapeutic vulnerability gradients, where Aumolertinib's dual‐progression subgroup (16.67%, 2/12) showed diminished LOT1 efficacy (PFS 1/10 months), contrasting with osimertinib's bifocal failure cohort (9.68%, 3/31) that maintained prolonged TKI sensitivity (PFS 7–38 months) before systemic deterioration (Figure 4D).

Post‐progression management exhibited marked intercohort divergence upon trial completion. In the aumolertinib cohort (*n* = 12), therapeutic strategies included protocol‐compliant continuation (16.7%, 2/12) and antiangiogenic‐immunotherapy combinations (16.7%, 2/12) comprising immunotherapy/bevacizumab/paclitaxel/carboplatin (8.3%, 1/12) and sintilimab/bevacizumab/pemetrexed‐carboplatin (8.3%, 1/12), while 66.7% (8/12) received unrecorded salvage therapies. The osimertinib cohort (*n* = 31) demonstrated greater therapeutic diversity: platinum‐based chemotherapy (16.1%, 5/31) including AP (cisplatin‐pemetrexed), bevacizumab‐enhanced AP/PC (pemetrexed‐carboplatin), and anti‐PD‐1/bevacizumab/PC combinations (3.2% each); osimertinib intensification (6.5%, 2/31) through 160 mg monotherapy (3.2%, 1/31) or dual EGFR/VEGFR inhibition with 160 mg osimertinib plus 12 mg anlotinib (3.2%, 1/31); multimodal targeted regimens (35.5%, 11/31) integrating osimertinib with bevacizumab (3.2%, 1/31), radiotherapy (6.5% total: 3.2% conventional, 3.2% bone metastasis‐directed), sintilimab (3.2%, 1/31), or anlotinib (3.2%, 1/31), alongside bevacizumab/paclitaxel‐platinum (3.2%, 1/31) and concurrent locoregional radiotherapy (3.2%, 1/31); subsequent‐line TKIs (9.7%, 3/31) including icotinib (3.2%, 1/31) and anlotinib (6.5%, 2/31); clinical trial participation (3.2%, 1/31); with 29.0% (9/31) pursuing undocumented therapeutic pathways.

## Discussion

This real‐world cohort study first demonstrates the survival advantage of aumolertinib over osimertinib in frontline treatment of EGFR‐mutant NSCLC, with a median progression‐free survival (mPFS) of 34.4 months versus 26.9 months (Δ7.5 months; HR 0.52, 95% CI 0.29–0.92, *p* = 0.031), corresponding to a 48% reduction in progression risk. These results substantially surpass those from phase III trials (osimertinib: 18.9 months in FLAURA; aumolertinib: 19.3 months in AENEAS), potentially attributable to real‐world flexible disease assessment strategies (symptom‐driven progression evaluation rather than fixed imaging schedules) and optimized supportive care [[11,12]]. Notably, the PFS benefit of aumolertinib was corroborated by Yang et al. (16 vs. 12 months, *p* = 0.045), suggesting cross‐study consistency in therapeutic efficacy [[13]].

Subgroup analyses identified baseline co‐mutation status as a critical efficacy determinant. The aumolertinib cohort exhibited significantly lower co‐mutation rates than the osimertinib group (16.98%, 9/53, vs. 35.71%, 20/56; *p* = 0.046), aligning with established evidence that TP53 co‐mutations markedly reduce mPFS (CTONG 0901: 14.5 vs. 10.7 months for wild‐type vs. mutant, *p* = 0.041; Tan et al.: 31.1 vs. 7.0 months, *p* < 0.001) [[14,15]]. However, several points support the robustness of our findings. Strikingly, the consistency of the HR = 0.51 in the co‐mutation‐negative subgroup (*n* = 80) mirrors that of the overall population, providing a strong supportive signal that the therapeutic benefit of aumolertinib persists in a balanced genomic background. Furthermore, while co‐mutated patients exhibited shortened mPFS in both arms, aumolertinib maintained a favorable numerical trend over osimertinib in this high‐risk subgroup (HR 0.68, 95% CI 0.25–1.84, *p* = 0.48). The lack of statistical significance here likely stems from limited statistical power (*n* = 29) and mutational heterogeneity beyond TP53. Nevertheless, given the retrospective nature of this study, these findings should be interpreted as exploratory and hypothesis‐generating, necessitating further validation in larger, prospective cohorts.

In EGFR‐mutant NSCLC patients with brain metastases, aumolertinib demonstrated a superior PFS advantage over osimertinib (HR 0.24, 95% CI 0.09–0.66, *p* = 0.006), accompanied by a 34% reduction in intracranial progression rate (8.33% vs. 12.9%) and delayed breakthrough progression to 30 months of frontline therapy. In contrast, osimertinib‐treated patients exhibited heterogeneous progression timelines (5–35 months), reflecting less predictable CNS control. Mechanistically, aumolertinib's cyclopropyl substitution at the indole N‐methyl position enhances BBB penetration efficiency by 3.2‐fold (cerebrospinal fluid/plasma concentration ratio), sustaining higher therapeutic concentrations in brain parenchyma. Notably, its major metabolite HAS‐719 lost BBB penetrability due to increased polarity, establishing an efficacy‐toxicity decoupling mechanism: structural optimization amplifies CNS drug exposure while metabolite pharmacokinetics circumvent neurotoxic accumulation. This dual action synergistically optimizes clinical outcomes, extending median PFS by 5.1 months and reducing neurotoxic AEs by 42% [[12]]. Such a targeted molecular design paradigm provides a therapeutic blueprint for EGFR‐mutant NSCLC with brain metastases, achieving both durable lesion control (30 months vs. 5–35 months heterogeneous distribution) and CNS safety [[13]].

Current comparative analyses of OS between aumolertinib and osimertinib in brain metastasis patients reveal distinct survival benefit mechanisms. Aumolertinib demonstrated groundbreaking efficacy in the Phase II APOLLO trial (China cohort), achieving a median OS of 30.2 months as second‐line therapy for EGFR T790M+ advanced NSCLC—the longest reported OS for third‐generation EGFR‐TKI monotherapy in Chinese populations. Although OS data from the Phase III AENEAS trial of aumolertinib in first‐line Chinese patients remain unpublished, its first‐line CNS mPFS was 29 months vs. gefitinib's 8.3 months (HR 0.319). Combined with 3.2‐fold higher BBB penetration than osimertinib, this suggests aumolertinib's potential advantage in controlling CNS lesions. In contrast, osimertinib showed a global median OS of 38.6 months in FLAURA (Chinese subgroup validated), and its FLAURA2 chemotherapy combination achieved CNS PFS of 30.2 months, yet brain metastasis subgroup OS remains immature, with potential underestimation of Chinese population heterogeneity in EGFR‐TKI responses. Notably, post‐resistance strategies diverge: aumolertinib's retained activity against C797S mutations enables sequential fourth‐generation EGFR‐TKI therapy, whereas it prolonged median PFS by 5.1 months as crossover therapy in osimertinib‐resistant T790M/C797S co‐mutations, underscoring its unique salvage potential. Critical limitations include absent head‐to‐head comparisons, immature aumolertinib OS data (APOLLO median follow‐up: 22.87 months), and extrapolation risks between global/regional cohorts, necessitating extended follow‐up, multinational trials, and dynamic resistance monitoring to clarify survival disparities. In this study, the aumolertinib group had nonassessable median OS (mOS), while osimertinib achieved mOS of 53.8 months (95% CI: 38.7–NA), with no significant intergroup difference (*p* > 0.05). Despite comparable median follow‐up durations (aumolertinib: 22.87 months, 95% CI: 19.63–29.87; osimertinib: 23.80 months, 95% CI: 17.19–33.26), aumolertinib's survival curve remained above the 50% event threshold, suggesting potential underestimation of its survival benefits.

The safety profiles of aumolertinib and osimertinib, as evidenced by cumulative evidence and this study, highlight clinically relevant distinctions with implications for therapeutic optimization. Aumolertinib demonstrates a favorable overall toxicity profile, characterized by lower rates of grade ≥ 3 AEs (7%,2/53 vs. 10.6%, 2/56) and reduced cardiac risks (e.g., QT prolongation incidence < 1%) compared to osimertinib, while osimertinib maintains a predictable safety pattern despite its higher propensity for ILD (4% vs. 0.9%) and diarrhea (60% vs. 16.4%), the latter potentially attributable to its active metabolite AZ5104. Notably, aumolertinib's enhanced BBB penetration (3.2‐fold higher than osimertinib) confers superior intracranial efficacy without exacerbating neurotoxicity in brain metastasis patients, contrasting with osimertinib's established but ILD‐prone profile in this population. The divergence extends to comorbid populations: aumolertinib's minimal myelosuppression and cardiovascular burden position as preferable for frail or elderly patients with cardiopulmonary vulnerabilities, whereas osimertinib remains contraindicated in those with preexisting pulmonary fibrosis. Region‐specific pharmacogenomic profiles further distinguish these agents: Aumolertinib's China‐developed properties were validated in the POLESTAR trial as consolidation therapy after definitive radiochemotherapy for unresectable stage III EGFR‐mutant NSCLC, demonstrating 0% ILD incidence and 30.4‐month mPFS, and in the AENEAS trial for first‐line advanced disease with 19.3‐month mPFS versus first‐generation EGFR‐TKIs at HR 0.46 [[1,4,7]]. Conversely, while osimertinib's LAURA trial showed significantly improved 24‐month PFS rates in Chinese locally advanced patients at 71% versus 8% with concurrent CRT, its Chinese subgroup maintained higher ILD incidence than aumolertinib at 8% versus 0%. Our findings reinforce these patterns, demonstrating comparable overall AE rates (37.76% vs. 39.29%, *p* > 0.05) while identifying osimertinib's persistent ILD‐associated radiographic anomalies (2.8% vs. 0.9%), a critical consideration for patients with subclinical pulmonary comorbidities. These results underscore aumolertinib's emerging role in safety‐sensitive contexts, particularly for Chinese populations and neuro‐oncological indications, while affirming osimertinib's global utility contingent on rigorous pulmonary monitoring. The collective evidence advocates precision‐based EGFR‐TKI selection, integrating metabolic profiles, regional genetic predispositions, and individualized risk–benefit assessments to optimize therapeutic outcomes.

Univariate regression analysis identified age < 65 years (HR 0.46, 95% CI 0.22–0.99, *p* = 0.046), brain metastases, and 19del mutations as independent predictors of prolonged PFS, with 19del carriers demonstrating superior prognostic outcomes, a finding consistent with the efficacy profile of osimertinib in younger patients and 19del populations observed in the FLAURA trial, suggesting these biomarkers modulate therapeutic sensitivity to influence clinical outcomes; further analysis revealed that aumolertinib conferred greater PFS benefits over osimertinib in 19del patients (HR 0.43, 95% CI 0.2–0.89, *p* = 0.023), with both agents achieving significantly higher 1‐year PFS rates in 19del versus L858R subgroups (aumolertinib: 37% vs. 11%; osimertinib: 31% vs. 17%), a trend validated in randomized controlled trials of first‐generation TKIs (median PFS: 24 vs. 10 months for 19del vs. L858R, *p* = 0.04) [[16]]. Mechanistically, these disparities involve L858R's higher co‐mutation frequency (52% vs. 36%, HR 1.87, *p* < 0.001) [[17]], structural stabilization differences in EGFR C‐terminal phosphorylation domains, variations in Akt/Erk1/2 signaling intensity and G1‐phase arrest efficacy, alongside 19del's unique dimerization‐independent pathway activation [[18,19]]. Regarding age‐related prognostic effects, while younger patients (< 65 years) derived enhanced benefits from aumolertinib, conflicting evidence emerges: the HOT2002 study reported comparable efficacy in elderly patients (≥ 75 years) receiving osimertinib with a median PFS of 19.4 months (95% CI 15.9–23.9), mirroring FLAURA outcomes in younger cohorts, whereas retrospective data showed drastically reduced mPFS (2.4 months) in elderly patients treated with first‐generation TKIs [[20]]. This paradox may stem from age‐related physiological declines, including impaired drug metabolism, compromised organ function, and comorbidity‐driven therapeutic tolerance, underscoring the necessity for universal EGFR testing across age groups and preferential use of third‐generation TKIs in mutation‐positive cases to optimize clinical decision‐making [[21,22]].

## Conclusion

As third‐generation EGFR‐TKIs, both aumolertinib and osimertinib demonstrate clinical efficacy in prolonging PFS for NSCLC, particularly through their central nervous system activity and intracranial lesion control in patients with brain metastases. Current evidence reveals a superior efficacy trend for aumolertinib over osimertinib in EGFR classical mutation‐positive patients (HR 0.43, 95% CI 0.2–0.89, *p* = 0.023), with a 1‐year PFS rate of 37% in the 19del subgroup compared to 31% for osimertinib. These findings, combined with molecular mechanistic distinctions in TKI sensitivity between mutation subtypes (e.g., enhanced dimerization‐independent signaling in 19del variants), provide supportive evidence and a clinical rationale for the potential prioritization of aumolertinib in the first‐line treatment of EGFR‐mutant NSCLC. Beyond efficacy, the safety profile, specifically hepatic function, is a cornerstone of long‐term clinical management and patient prognosis. In our study, aumolertinib demonstrated a favorable hepatic safety profile with a lower overall incidence of liver dysfunction compared to osimertinib (9.43%,5/53 vs. 14.29%,8/56) and a low rate of Grade ≥ 3 events (1.89%,1/53). Given that stable liver function is essential for maintaining treatment intensity and improving overall outcomes in cancer patients, this manageable toxicity profile further underscores the role of aumolertinib as a balanced and safe therapeutic option. However, the retrospective design of this study introduces potential selection bias, while a limited sample size may compromise statistical power. The shorter follow‐up duration in the aumolertinib cohort likely underestimates long‐term efficacy differences due to insufficient accumulation of end point events (e.g., disease progression or death), and elevated censoring rates may reduce survival analysis precision. Future prospective studies with expanded cohorts, extended follow‐up, and integration of dynamic biomarker monitoring (e.g., circulating tumor DNA) coupled with pharmacokinetic profiling are warranted to validate these findings and refine predictive models for treatment optimization.
