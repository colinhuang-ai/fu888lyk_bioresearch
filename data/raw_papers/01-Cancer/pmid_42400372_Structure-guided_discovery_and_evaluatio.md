# Structure-guided discovery and evaluation of an EGFR L858R-targeting peptide with antiproliferative activity against ovarian cancer cells

## Abstract

EGFR L858R is an activating mutation associated with aberrant EGFR signalling, and molecules capable of recognising this mutant remain of research interest. In this study, a virtual peptide library containing 59 319 heptapeptides was screened against the EGFR L858R crystal structure, leading to the identification of four peptides with favourable predicted binding ability. Among them, Peptide-1 showed the strongest binding affinity in MST assays (*K*<sub>d</sub> = 0.35 ± 0.02 μM), with higher affinity than the reference peptide DTP-1. Integrated structural and dynamic analyses showed that Peptide-1 could form a relatively stable binding conformation with EGFR L858R. Peptide-1 reduced the viability of SKOV3, OVCAR3, and CaOV3 cells, showed weaker effects on IOSE-80 cells, and its activity was markedly attenuated after EGFR-targeting shRNA transduction. In addition, Peptide-1 decreased Cyclin D1 mRNA expression and increased Caspase-3 mRNA expression. These findings indicate that Peptide-1 can be further investigated as an EGFR L858R-targeting peptide.

## Introduction

Ovarian cancer is one of the major causes of cancer-related death among women worldwide[1,2]. Owing to the lack of effective screening methods, early diagnosis remains challenging, and approximately 80% of patients are diagnosed at an advanced stage[3]. Pathologically, more than 90% of these cases are classified as epithelial ovarian carcinomas[4]. Patients with early-stage disease can achieve a 5-year overall survival rate of 70%–95% after surgery combined with adjuvant platinum-based chemotherapy[5]. In contrast, patients with advanced-stage disease have a poorer prognosis. Although cytoreductive surgery, platinum-based chemotherapy, and targeted pharmacological intervention are available, approximately 75% of patients with advanced-stage disease relapse within 2 years, and the 5-year overall survival rate is only 10%–40%[5,6]. Current clinical approaches remain limited by drug resistance, recurrence, and off-target toxicity[7]. New targeted agents, such as poly(ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitors, also face limitations including limited durability of benefit and high cost[8,9]. Therefore, the exploration of new molecular targets and intervention strategies remains valuable for suppressing ovarian cancer progression.

Epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) is critically involved in regulating physiological processes, including epithelial cell proliferation, differentiation, and survival[10]. Aberrant EGFR expression or mutation may be associated with ovarian cancer progression and poor prognosis[11]. The L858R point mutation in exon 21 of EGFR is a typical activating mutation, characterised by the substitution of leucine with arginine at position 858, usually caused by a thymine-to-guanine transversion[12]. This mutation can prevent the activation loop segment from adopting the inactive helical conformation, thereby favouring maintenance of the EGFR kinase domain in an active state and markedly enhancing the kinase activity of mutant EGFR[13,14]. Previous studies have reported the detection of oncogenic EGFR L858R mutations in clinical ovarian cancer samples[15]. In addition, aberrant EGFR activation can promote ovarian cancer cell proliferation[16]. Related experimental studies have shown that inhibition of EGFR signalling can downregulate Cyclin D1 expression and increase Caspase-3 levels, suggesting restricted cell-cycle progression accompanied by apoptotic activation, thereby suppressing ovarian cancer cell proliferation[17,18].

A number of inhibitors targeting the EGFR L858R mutation have been reported, among which EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors (EGFR-TKIs) are the most representative. The first-generation EGFR-TKI gefitinib inhibits EGFR kinase activation by competing for the ATP-binding site[19], but its clinical use is often associated with EGFR-related adverse effects, such as rash, dry skin, and diarrhoea[20]. In contrast, the third-generation EGFR-TKI osimertinib shows stronger activity and broader mutation coverage against EGFR-sensitizing mutations and the T790M resistance mutation[21]. However, secondary resistance remains difficult to completely avoid[22]. Compared with conventional ATP-competitive EGFR-TKIs, peptide-based targeting molecules have advantages such as high structural tuneability and broader recognition interfaces, providing a differentiated strategy for targeting the EGFR L858R mutant[23]. Previous studies have reported STHHYYP as an EGFR L858R-targeting peptide, which was further developed into the dual-labelled molecular probe STHHYYP-ECGK-TAMRA<sup>24</sup>. In the present study, this molecule was designated as DTP-1. DTP-1 showed specific binding to EGFR L858R-positive NCI-H1975 cells, with a *K*<sub>d</sub> value of 130.6 ± 29.2 nM, and exhibited substantial mutant EGFR-targeted accumulation in an in vivo tumour model, suggesting the potential utility of peptide scaffolds for EGFR L858R mutant recognition[24]. Therefore, the development of new EGFR L858R-targeting molecules with favourable mutant-recognition ability may provide a new research direction for addressing the selectivity limitations of conventional EGFR-TKIs.

Computational simulation-assisted virtual screening can efficiently evaluate compound libraries and has become an important approach in hit identification and bioactive molecule discovery[25,26]. Molecular docking predicts ligand-target binding modes and spatial conformations and evaluates potential binding ability through scoring functions[27], thereby helping prioritise high-affinity molecules[28]. The integration of virtual screening and molecular docking can improve screening efficiency and provide complementary support for hit prioritisation[29]. In our previous studies, several bioactive molecules were identified using a virtual combinatorial screening strategy[30–32]. Based on this foundation, the present study further applied this approach to screen a peptide library against the EGFR L858R mutant and identified four new hit peptides with favourable predicted binding ability, designated Peptides 1–4. Among these peptides, Peptide-1 showed the strongest binding affinity towards the EGFR L858R mutant and reduced ovarian cancer cell viability, providing a basis for further investigation of EGFR L858R-targeting peptide molecules.

## Materials and methods

### General chemistry

Peptides 1–4, DTP-1 and the scrambled peptide were purchased from GL Biochem (Shanghai, China). Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) was obtained from Aladdin Reagent (Shanghai, China). Phosphate buffered saline (PBS), Roswell Park Memorial Institute 1640 medium (RPMI-1640), foetal bovine serum (FBS), and penicillin/streptomycin mixture were all acquired from Gibco (Grand Island, New York, USA).

### Cell lines and culture conditions

Human ovarian cancer cells SKOV3 (catalog number: HTB-77), OVCAR3 (catalog number: HTB-161) and CaOV3 (catalog number: HTB-75) were obtained from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC, Manassas, VA, USA). Normal human ovarian cells IOSE-80 (catalog number: AE-H049) were obtained from Shanghai Yubo Biotechnology Co., Ltd (Shanghai, China). All cell lines were grown in RPMI-1640 medium supplemented with 10% foetal bovine serum (FBS), 80 U/mL penicillin, and 0.08 mg/mL streptomycin under humidified conditions at 37 °C and 5% CO<sub>2</sub>. The cells were passaged every 2–3 days. For all cellular experiments in this study, single-cell suspensions were prepared using cells in the logarithmic growth phase.

### Virtual peptide library construction

The method for the construction of the peptide library was adopted from published literature[30]. Using the QuaSAR-CombiGen module embedded in the Molecular Operating Environment (MOE; Chemical Computing Group Inc., Montreal, Quebec, Canada), full combinatorial enumeration was performed on three sets of peptide fragment libraries to construct a virtual compound library containing 59 319 peptide molecules. Each peptide molecule in the library is composed of seven amino acids. Specifically, the linkage sites between peptide fragments were defined as terminal atoms designated as “An” (where “n” is a positive integer). For the first dipeptide fragment set, the oxygen atom at the C-terminus was defined as the “A0” site, yielding a total of 39 fragments. For the second dipeptide fragment set, the hydrogen atom at the N-terminus and the oxygen atom at the C-terminus were assigned as the “A1” and “A2” sites, respectively, generating 39 fragments. For the third tripeptide fragment set, the hydrogen atom at the N-terminus was defined as the “A0” site, with 39 fragments obtained. The “A0” sites of the first and third peptide fragment sets were linked to the “A1” and “A2” sites of the second fragment set, respectively. Full combinatorial enumeration of the three fragment sets ultimately generated a virtual library of 59 319 heptapeptides. The resulting two-dimensional (2D) structures were subsequently converted into three-dimensional (3D) structures in MOE and subjected to energy minimisation for subsequent molecular docking studies.

### EGFR L858R mutant crystal structure preprocessing

Crystal structure preprocessing was performed as previously described[33]. The crystal structure of the EGFR L858R (PDB ID: 5CZI; resolution: 2.60 Å) was obtained from the Protein Data Bank (PDB). The structure was imported into MOE and prepared using the QuickPrep workflow with the Amber10:EHT force field. Hydrogen atoms were assigned using Protonate 3D, and water molecules located more than 4.5 Å from the protein structure were removed. The resulting structure was subjected to energy minimisation until the RMS gradient reached 0.1 kcal·mol<sup>−1</sup>·Å <sup>− 1</sup>.

### Virtual screening

Virtual screening was performed following the strategy reported in the literature[30]. The preprocessed EGFR L858R structure was used as the receptor for docking. The original residues in the binding pocket were retained, and no additional mutations were introduced. Protonation states were assigned at physiological pH and verified using Protonate 3D in MOE. The binding site for docking was defined with reference to the position of the co-crystallized ligand, and a spherical docking region with a radius of 5 Å was generated to encompass the binding pocket. During docking, peptide conformations were generated using the Triangle Matcher placement method, with up to 100 poses generated for each peptide. The receptor was kept rigid, while the peptides were allowed to remain flexible. Initial scoring was performed using the London dG scoring function, followed by rescoring with the GBVI/WSA dG method. All calculations were repeated three times under identical conditions, and the mean predicted binding free energy, namely the docking score, was used for peptide prioritisation. The four top-ranked peptides were selected for subsequent experimental evaluation.

In addition, the top-ranked peptide was randomly scrambled to generate a scrambled peptide. The scrambled peptide had the same amino acid composition and length as the original peptide but a different sequence order, and was used as a negative control in subsequent experiments to assess non-specific effects.

### Microscale thermophoresis (MST) assay

The binding affinities of Peptides 1–4, DTP-1, and the scrambled control peptide towards EGFR L858R were measured using microscale thermophoresis (MST), following previously reported procedures[34]. First, the EGFR L858R protein was fluorescently labelled using the Monolith<sup>TM</sup> RED-NHS 2nd Generation Protein Labelling Kit (NanoTemper Technologies GmbH, Munich, Germany). After labelling, the tube containing the fluorescently labelled protein was wrapped in aluminium foil and kept on ice to protect it from light and maintain protein activity and fluorescence stability. Subsequently, Peptides 1–4, DTP-1, and the scrambled control peptide were dissolved in PBST buffer and serially diluted. The fluorescently labelled EGFR L858R protein was then incubated with different concentrations of each peptide for 5 min in the dark to allow sufficient protein-peptide binding. Finally, the samples were loaded into capillaries, and temperature gradient-induced changes in fluorescence signals were measured using a Monolith NT.115 instrument (NanoTemper Technologies GmbH).

### Structure-activity relationship (SAR) analysis

The structure-activity relationship (SAR) analysis was performed according to the previously published literature[35]. To better characterise the interactions between the peptides and EGFR L858R, MOE was used to visualise the three-dimensional binding pocket and binding modes of Peptides 1–4 within the active site of EGFR L858R.

### Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations

The experimental methodology employed in this study was consistent with that reported in the literature[32]. Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations were performed using GROMACS 2021.5 to evaluate the structural stability of the EGFR L858R-Peptide-1 complex. The initial complex structure was obtained from the docking model generated in MOE. The EGFR L858R topology was generated using the AMBER99SB-ILDN force field, whereas the topology of Peptide-1 was generated using the ACPYPE server after ligand preprocessing. The complex was solvated with the SPC water model in a cubic simulation box under periodic boundary conditions, with a minimum distance of 1.0 nm maintained between the complex and the box boundary. Na<sup>+</sup> and Cl<sup>−</sup> ions were added to neutralise the system. Energy minimisation was performed using the steepest descent algorithm to remove unfavourable steric contacts. The system was then equilibrated by sequential 100 ps NVT and 100 ps NPT simulations. Temperature was maintained at 300 K using the V-rescale thermostat, and pressure was maintained at 1 bar using the Parrinello-Rahman barostat. After equilibration, a 50 ns production MD simulation was performed on the BeiKun Cloud platform, with trajectory frames saved every 10 ps. Trajectory analyses were conducted to evaluate the conformational stability of the complex, including the root-mean-square deviation (RMSD), root-mean-square fluctuation (RMSF), radius of gyration (Rg), hydrogen-bond number, and secondary-structure changes. The resulting data were plotted using GraphPad Prism 10.0 (GraphPad Software Inc., San Diego, CA, USA).

### Free energy landscape

Based on the RMSD and Rg data of the EGFR L858R-Peptide-1 complex, Gibbs free energy landscape data were generated via GROMACS, with 2D and 3D visualisation analyses conducted using Python (version 3.13.3).

### MM/PBSA calculations

Molecular mechanics/Poisson-Boltzmann surface area (MM/PBSA) is widely applied to estimate protein-ligand binding free energies. Following previously reported strategies[36], the binding free energy of the EGFR L858R-Peptide-1 complex was calculated using the single-trajectory approach. The binding free energy was calculated as the free-energy difference between the EGFR L858R-Peptide-1 complex and the combined unbound EGFR L858R receptor and Peptide-1 ligand. This method was used to quantitatively evaluate the predicted binding tendency between Peptide-1 and EGFR L858R.

### MTT-based cell viability assay

The experimental procedure was performed as previously described[30]. SKOV3, OVCAR3, and CaOV3 human ovarian cancer cells, together with the normal ovarian epithelial cell line IOSE-80, were plated in 96-well plates at a density of 5 × 10<sup>3</sup> cells per well in 100 μL of RPMI-1640 medium and incubated overnight to allow cell attachment. The cells were then treated with Peptides 1–4, DTP-1, or the scrambled peptide using two-fold serial dilutions. After 72 h of incubation at 37 °C under humidified conditions with 5% CO<sub>2</sub>, each well was treated with 10 μL of MTT solution (5 mg/mL in PBS) and incubated for a further 4 h. The culture medium was then removed, followed by the addition of 100 μL DMSO to solubilise the formazan crystals. Absorbance at 570 nm was recorded using a microplate reader (BioTek Synergy HT), and half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC<sub>50</sub>) values were determined from the cell viability data. All experiments were conducted in triplicate and independently repeated at least three times.

### Lentiviral shRNA-mediated EGFR knockdown

The experimental procedures were performed according to previously published methods[37]. For EGFR knockdown, lentiviral particles carrying EGFR-targeting shRNA or non-targeting control shRNA were diluted in Opti-MEM medium (Gibco, Grand Island, NY, USA) containing 6 μg/mL polybrene and then added to SKOV3 cells. After viral transduction, cells were selected using 5 μg/mL puromycin. The selected shControl and shEGFR SKOV3 cells were then seeded into 96-well plates and treated with Peptide-1 or the scrambled peptide. Cell viability was assessed using the MTT assay, and IC<sub>50</sub> values were calculated from the dose-response data.

### Reverse transcription quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) assay

RT-qPCR was performed to measure the mRNA expression levels of Cyclin D1 and Caspase-3. Experimental procedures were carried out as previously described[38,39]. SKOV3 cells treated with 2 μM or 10 μM Peptide-1 for 24 h were washed twice with PBS, followed by centrifugation at 13 000 rpm to obtain the cell pellets. Total RNA was extracted from the pellets using TRIzol reagent (cat. no. 15596026, Invitrogen, USA). cDNA was synthesised from 1 μg of total RNA using the High-Capacity cDNA Reverse Transcription Kit (cat. no. 4374966, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). qPCR was subsequently performed on an Applied Biosystems 7500 Real-Time PCR System using SYBR Green qPCR Master Mix (Thermo Fisher Scientific). The primer sequences were as follows: Cyclin D1 forward, 5′-AAGCCGCTTTTCCACGGCGA-3′ and reverse, 5′-AGGCTCCCCGGCTTCCACTT-3′; Caspase-3 forward, 5′-CTCTGGTTTTCGGTGGGTGT-3′ and reverse, 5′-CGCTTCCATGTATGATCTTTGGTT-3′. GAPDH was used as the reference gene. The qPCR conditions included an initial denaturation at 95 °C for 30 s, followed by 40 amplification cycles comprising denaturation at 95 °C for 10 s, annealing at 60 °C for 60 s, and extension at 72 °C for 20 s. Relative expression of the target mRNAs was calculated by the 2<sup>−ΔΔCt</sup> method and presented as fold changes.

### Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism 10.0 (GraphPad Software Inc., San Diego, CA, USA). Differences among groups were analysed by one-way analysis of variance (one-way ANOVA). A *p* values < 0.05 was considered statistically significant (**p* < 0.05, ***p* < 0.01, p* < 0.001). Data are presented as mean ± SD. Each experiment was performed at least three times.

## Results and discussion

### Identification of EGFR L858R-binding peptides by virtual screening

The virtual screening workflow for EGFR L858R-binding peptides is shown in Figure 1. In this study, a combined virtual screening and molecular docking strategy was used to identify peptides with favourable predicted binding ability towards EGFR L858R from a library consisting of 59 319 peptides. Each peptide in the library was individually docked into the binding pocket of the prepared EGFR L858R crystal structure. As shown in Figure 2, all peptides were ranked according to their docking scores, defined as the predicted binding free energies, from the most favourable to the least favourable values. A lower predicted binding free energy is indicative of a more favourable theoretical binding between the peptide and its target protein. The top four peptides, designated Peptides 1–4, were selected for subsequent interaction analysis. Among them, Peptide-1 exhibited the lowest predicted binding free energy, suggesting the most favourable theoretical binding ability towards EGFR L858R in the docking model. A SciFinder literature search indicated that Peptides 1–4 had not been previously reported; therefore, they were defined as new peptides in this study. In addition, the amino acid sequence of Peptide-1 was randomly scrambled to construct the corresponding scrambled control peptide.

> **Figure 1.** Workflow for the discovery and validation of EGFR L858R-targeting peptide Peptide-1. The EGFR L858R crystal structure (PDB ID: 5CZI) was prepared and used for virtual screening of a self-constructed peptide library containing 59 319 peptides. The peptides were prioritised according to their docking scores, followed by MST binding validation, SAR analysis, molecular dynamics simulation, and cell-based evaluation. Peptide-1 showed favourable binding affinity towards EGFR L858R (*K*<sub>d</sub> = 0.35 ± 0.02 μM) and antiproliferative activity in SKOV3 cells (IC<sub>50</sub> = 0.77 ± 0.04 μM), and was selected as the main peptide for further investigation.

### MST validation of the binding affinity of Peptides 1–4 towards EGFR L858R

To validate the experimental binding ability of the peptides identified by virtual screening towards EGFR L858R, MST assays were performed to determine the dissociation constants (*K*<sub>d</sub>) of Peptides 1–4 and the reference peptide DTP-1. As shown in Table 1, Peptides 1–4 exhibited distinct binding affinities towards EGFR L858R. Among them, Peptide-1 (DASYpYAD-NH<sub>2</sub>) showed the strongest binding ability, with a *K*<sub>d</sub> value of 0.35 ± 0.02 μM. This affinity was higher than that of the reference peptide DTP-1 (*K*<sub>d</sub> = 0.71 ± 0.05 μM), suggesting that Peptide-1 had more favourable binding characteristics towards EGFR L858R in this experimental system. In contrast, Peptide-2 and Peptide-3 showed gradually reduced binding affinities, with *K*<sub>d</sub> values of 2.41 ± 0.13 μM and 6.08 ± 0.52 μM, respectively. Peptide-4 had a *K*<sub>d</sub> value of 18.17 ± 0.96 μM, indicating relatively weak binding affinity towards EGFR L858R. Overall, the MST results were generally consistent with the virtual screening ranking and supported the selection of Peptide-1 as the main peptide for subsequent interaction analysis and cell-based evaluation.

### SAR analysis of the predicted binding interactions between Peptides 1–4 and EGFR L858R

To analyse the predicted binding interactions between the EGFR L858R mutant and Peptides 1–4, the three-dimensional binding modes of Peptides 1–4 within the EGFR L858R binding pocket were visualised using MOE (Figure 3). The hydrogen-bonding and hydrophobic interactions identified from the docking models are summarised in Table 2. All peptides formed multiple hydrogen bonds with Gly874, Val876, Pro877, Lys879, Arg889, Lys913, and Ala920, suggesting that these residues may constitute the major interaction region for peptide recognition of the EGFR L858R mutant. In addition, all peptides formed hydrophobic interactions with Ile878. Notably, compared with Peptide-4, the terminal Asp residue of Peptide-3 formed two additional hydrogen bonds with Arg889. Compared with Peptide-3, the Ala residue in Peptide-2 formed one hydrogen bond with Arg841. Furthermore, compared with Peptide-2, Peptide-1 formed an additional hydrogen bond with Asp837. Overall, Peptide-1 formed the highest number of hydrogen bonds and displayed the most complete polar interaction network, suggesting that Peptide-1 may possess the most favourable predicted binding mode among the four hit peptides.

### MD Simulation analysis on dynamic stability of the EGFR L858R-Peptide-1 complex

To evaluate the dynamic stability of the EGFR L858R-Peptide-1 complex, a 50 ns MD simulation was performed, and RMSD, RMSF, Rg, hydrogen-bond number, and secondary-structure changes were analysed. As shown in Figure 4(A), the complex RMSD gradually rose during the early simulation stage and showed relatively greater fluctuations around 10–15 ns. Thereafter, the RMSD gradually decreased and stabilised within the range of approximately 0.22–0.28 nm, suggesting that the complex reached a relatively stable conformational state during the later stage of the simulation. Residue fluctuation analysis showed that most residues of EGFR L858R had low RMSF values, mainly below 0.30 nm, with higher fluctuations observed only at the terminal regions and a few flexible segments (Figure 4(B)). This indicates that the overall structure remained stable while retaining limited local flexibility. As shown in Figure 4(C), the Rg value remained within a narrow range of approximately 2.00–2.06 nm throughout the simulation, suggesting that the protein maintained a compact overall conformation without obvious unfolding. Hydrogen-bond analysis showed that approximately 6–10 hydrogen bonds were continuously formed between EGFR L858R and Peptide-1, with most time points maintaining around 7–8 hydrogen bonds (Figure 4(D)), indicating that a stable hydrogen-bonding network contributed to complex stability. Secondary-structure analysis further showed that the major secondary-structure components of EGFR L858R remained broadly stable during the 50 ns simulation, with no obvious structural transition observed (Figure 4(E)). Taken together, these results suggest that the EGFR L858R-Peptide-1 complex maintained favourable conformational stability under the simulated conditions.

### Binding energy analysis of the EGFR L858R-Peptide-1 complex

To further characterise the binding features of the EGFR L858R-Peptide-1 complex, per-residue energy decomposition and binding free energy calculations were performed using the MM/PBSA method (Figure 4(F,G) and Table 3). Residue contribution analysis showed that several residues within the EGFR L858R binding pocket made favourable contributions to Peptide-1 binding. Among them, Arg841, Val876, Lys879, and Arg889 showed relatively pronounced negative energy contributions, suggesting that they may represent potential hotspot residues involved in maintaining Peptide-1 binding. In addition, Lys875, Pro877, Ile878, Trp880, and Lys913 also showed certain favourable contributions. Energy component decomposition further indicated that the net energy contribution of each residue was mainly determined by the balance between electrostatic interactions and polar solvation energy, whereas van der Waals interactions and nonpolar solvation terms made relatively limited contributions (Figure 4(G)). For charged residues such as Arg841, Lys875, Lys879, Arg889, and Lys913, favourable electrostatic interactions may contribute to stabilising the binding conformation of Peptide-1 within the EGFR L858R binding pocket. Consistently, MM/PBSA total energy analysis showed that the predicted total binding free energy of Peptide-1 with EGFR L858R was −37.97 kcal/mol (Table 3), indicating a favourable binding tendency of the complex in the computational model. Among the calculated energy components, electrostatic energy (−337.31 kcal/mol) and van der Waals energy (−42.82 kcal/mol) provided the main favourable contributions. Overall, the binding of Peptide-1 to EGFR L858R may be mainly supported by a charged residue-mediated polar interaction network, accompanied by certain hydrophobic and van der Waals interaction contributions.

### Free energy landscape of the EGFR L858R-Peptide-1 complex

To further evaluate the conformational stability of the EGFR L858R-Peptide-1 complex during the simulation, a Gibbs free energy landscape (FEL) was constructed based on RMSD and Rg values (Figure 5). The two-dimensional contour map and three-dimensional surface plot showed that the low-energy regions of the complex were mainly concentrated within an RMSD range of approximately 0.20–0.28 nm and an Rg range of approximately 2.00–2.04 nm (Figure 5(A,B)). The relatively concentrated blue low-energy basin suggests that the EGFR L858R-Peptide-1 complex tended to occupy a stable conformational space during the 50 ns MD simulation. Consistent with the RMSD and Rg analyses, no obvious conformational divergence or global unfolding was observed. These results further support that Peptide-1 can form a relatively stable binding conformation with EGFR L858R under the simulated conditions.

### Peptide-1 exhibits sequence-related antiproliferative activity and an apparent selectivity trend in ovarian cancer cells

To evaluate whether the antiproliferative activity of Peptide-1 was associated with its sequence and EGFR-related binding properties, and to assess whether non-specific cytotoxicity contributed to the observed activity, the activities of Peptides 1–4 and the reference peptide DTP-1 were first compared in SKOV3 cells. As shown in Table 1, Peptide-1 exhibited the strongest antiproliferative activity in SKOV3 cells, with an IC<sub>50</sub> value of 0.77 ± 0.04 μM, which was lower than that of DTP-1 (IC<sub>50</sub> = 1.54 ± 0.22 μM). In contrast, Peptide-2 and Peptide-3 showed markedly reduced activity, with IC<sub>50</sub> values of 4.09 ± 0.26 μM and 9.15 ± 0.48 μM, respectively, whereas Peptide-4 showed no obvious activity within the tested concentration range (IC<sub>50</sub> > 10 μM). The activity of Peptide-1 was further evaluated in other ovarian cancer cells and normal ovarian epithelial cells. As shown in Table 4, Peptide-1 also exhibited low-micromolar antiproliferative activity against OVCAR3 and CaOV3 cells, with IC<sub>50</sub> values of 1.14 ± 0.07 μM and 0.86 ± 0.05 μM, respectively, whereas its IC<sub>50</sub> value in normal ovarian epithelial IOSE-80 cells was >10 μM, suggesting a relatively weak effect on normal cells. Meanwhile, the scrambled control peptide showed no obvious activity in OVCAR3, CaOV3, or IOSE-80 cells, with IC<sub>50</sub> values >10 μM in all three cell lines. Taken together, these results indicate that Peptide-1 exhibited appreciable antiproliferative activity in the tested ovarian cancer cells and showed an apparent selectivity trend over normal ovarian epithelial cells. Its activity may be associated with its specific amino acid sequence and EGFR-related binding properties, rather than being solely attributable to non-specific cytotoxicity.

### EGFR-targeting shRNA transduction attenuates the antiproliferative activity of Peptide-1 in SKOV3 cells

To assess whether the antiproliferative activity of Peptide-1 was related to EGFR expression, SKOV3 cells were transduced with EGFR-targeting shRNA or non-targeting control shRNA, and the activity of Peptide-1 was evaluated in the shControl and shEGFR groups. As shown in Table 5, Peptide-1 exhibited evident antiproliferative activity in shControl SKOV3 cells, with an IC<sub>50</sub> value of 0.77 ± 0.04 μM. However, after EGFR-targeting shRNA transduction and puromycin selection, the antiproliferative effect of Peptide-1 was markedly reduced, with the IC<sub>50</sub> value increasing to >10 μM. Meanwhile, the scrambled control peptide showed no obvious activity in either shControl or shEGFR cells, with IC<sub>50</sub> values >10 μM in both groups. Taken together, these results suggest that the growth-inhibitory effect of Peptide-1 may be associated with EGFR expression and its specific amino acid sequence, rather than being solely attributable to non-specific cytotoxicity.

### Peptide-1 regulates Cyclin D1 and caspase-3 mRNA expression in SKOV3 cells

To further evaluate the effect of Peptide-1 on Cyclin D1 and Caspase-3 expression in SKOV3 cells, RT-qPCR was performed to measure their relative mRNA levels. As shown in Figure 6, compared with the control group, Peptide-1 treatment reduced the relative mRNA expression of the cell-cycle-related gene Cyclin D1, showing a concentration-related trend. After treatment with 2 μM Peptide-1, the relative mRNA level of Cyclin D1 was approximately 0.5-fold that of the control group; when the concentration was increased to 10 μM, it further decreased to approximately 0.2 to 0.3 times the control level. In contrast, the relative mRNA expression of Caspase-3 was increased, reaching approximately 1.5-fold and 2.0-fold of the control level after treatment with 2 μM and 10 μM Peptide-1, respectively. These changes were statistically significant compared with the control group (p* < 0.001). Taken together, these results indicate that Peptide-1 decreased Cyclin D1 mRNA expression and increased Caspase-3 mRNA expression in SKOV3 cells, suggesting that its inhibitory effect on SKOV3 cell viability may be associated with reduced Cyclin D1 expression and increased Caspase-3 transcription.

## Conclusion

In this study, an integrated strategy combining computational simulation and experimental validation was used to screen and identify EGFR L858R-targeting peptides from a self-constructed peptide library containing 59 319 heptapeptides. Virtual screening and molecular docking indicated that Peptides 1–4 showed favourable predicted binding ability towards EGFR L858R, with Peptide-1 exhibiting the lowest predicted binding free energy. MST assays further demonstrated binding between Peptide-1 and EGFR L858R, with a *K*<sub>d</sub> value of 0.35 ± 0.02 μM, indicating higher binding affinity than the reference peptide DTP-1. These results indicate that Peptide-1 had favourable binding affinity towards EGFR L858R in this experimental system. SAR analysis showed that Peptide-1 formed a relatively complete hydrogen-bonding network with residues including Gly874, Val876, Pro877, Lys879, Arg889, Lys913, and Ala920 within the EGFR L858R binding pocket, together with a hydrophobic interaction involving Ile878. These residues may participate in the recognition of EGFR L858R by Peptide-1.

Further 50 ns MD simulation, Gibbs free energy landscape analysis, and MM/PBSA calculations supported the conformational stability of the EGFR L858R-Peptide-1 complex under the simulated conditions. RMSD, Rg, hydrogen-bond number, and secondary-structure analyses showed that the complex remained relatively stable during the later stage of the simulation. FEL analysis indicated that the low-energy conformations were mainly concentrated within an RMSD range of approximately 0.20–0.28 nm and an Rg range of approximately 2.00–2.04 nm. MM/PBSA analysis showed that the predicted total binding free energy of Peptide-1 with EGFR L858R was −37.97 kcal/mol, with electrostatic and van der Waals interactions providing the main favourable contributions. These computational results further support a favourable binding tendency between Peptide-1 and EGFR L858R from the perspectives of conformational stability and energetic contribution.

In cellular assays, Peptide-1 exhibited low-micromolar antiproliferative activity in SKOV3, OVCAR3, and CaOV3 ovarian cancer cells, with an IC<sub>50</sub> value of 0.77 ± 0.04 μM in SKOV3 cells, which was lower than that of the reference peptide DTP-1. In contrast, Peptide-1 showed a weaker effect on normal ovarian epithelial IOSE-80 cells, and the scrambled control peptide showed no obvious activity in the tested cells. These findings suggest that the cellular activity of Peptide-1 may be associated with its specific amino acid sequence rather than being solely attributable to non-specific cytotoxicity. After EGFR-targeting shRNA transduction, the antiproliferative activity of Peptide-1 in SKOV3 cells was markedly attenuated, further suggesting that its cellular activity may be related to EGFR expression. In addition, Peptide-1 reduced the relative mRNA expression level of Cyclin D1 and increased that of Caspase-3, suggesting that its inhibitory effect on SKOV3 cell viability may be associated with reduced Cyclin D1 expression and increased Caspase-3 transcription. Overall, this study identifies Peptide-1 as a peptide molecule with EGFR L858R-binding ability in biochemical assays and antiproliferative activity in ovarian cancer cells, providing a basis for further structural optimisation and functional evaluation of EGFR L858R-targeting peptides.
